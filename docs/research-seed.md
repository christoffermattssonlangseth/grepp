# Evidence

Findings the coach programmes from. One per line; the tag is how it cites them.

Copy this file to your training repo as `research.md` (or whatever path you set in
Settings ▸ Coach ▸ evidence file). Every entry below was written from the
model's memory of the literature, not from the papers themselves: check each
one against the paper before you lean on it, prune what you don't agree with,
and add your own by handing the coach an abstract or a DOI.

- [R1] Ten or more weekly sets per muscle produced roughly twice the hypertrophy of fewer than five; the dose-response was graded, not a step. — Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, J Sports Sci. doi:10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197
- [R2] Training a muscle twice a week beat once a week for hypertrophy; whether three beats two was unclear. — Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016, Sports Med. doi:10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8
- [R3] Low loads (over 15 reps) and high loads grew similar muscle when sets were taken near failure; heavy loads produced larger 1RM gains. — Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, J Strength Cond Res. doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200
- [R4] Three-minute rests beat one-minute rests for both strength and hypertrophy in trained men over eight weeks. — Schoenfeld et al. 2016, J Strength Cond Res. doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000001272
- [R5] Training to failure was not necessary for hypertrophy; sets stopped a few reps short produced similar growth, and failure did not add strength. — Refalo et al. 2023, Sports Med. doi:10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y
- [R6] For strength, several sets per exercise beat one, and about five or more weekly sets per exercise beat fewer. — Ralston et al. 2017, Sports Med. doi:10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7
- [R7] With weekly volume equated, spreading it over more days did not add strength; frequency matters mainly as a way to fit volume in. — Grgic et al. 2018, Sports Med. doi:10.1007/s40279-018-0872-x
- [R8] Meta-regression across studies found hypertrophy still rising past 20 weekly sets per muscle, with each added set worth less than the last; strength gains flattened much earlier. — Pelland et al. 2024, preprint (SportRxiv)
